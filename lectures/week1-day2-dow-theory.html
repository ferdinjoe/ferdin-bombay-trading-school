Market Structure Course - Week 1, Day 2

Dow Theory: price, the three trends,
and volume that has to agree

Read on the board as doubt theory - it is Dow Theory, after Charles H. Dow, who wrote the market letters in the 1890s. Nothing in today's session is a new invention. It is the oldest filter we have, and it is the reason this course refuses to call an unconfirmed move a signal.

1. Price discounts everything

And the close is the loudest part of price. Election day, Nifty Auto, the 2020 lockdown - all real tape.

2. Three trends

Primary on monthly, secondary for 18-24 months, minor under 3 weeks. Trade the minor only in the direction of the primary.

3. Volume must confirm

A break of a prior high or low that volume does not support is a rumour, not a signal.

Dow Theory - the three things we test today

Three claims. All three are falsifiable on a chart.

The index is a thermometer

Dow: the average discounts (absorbs) everything that is known and most of what is merely rumoured. If information exists, somebody with money has already traded it.

Trends come in three sizes

Primary (years, monthly chart), secondary (weeks to ~2 years, the correction inside the big move), minor (under three weeks, the noise you trade or ignore).

Volume must confirm the trend

Dow's own test: participation should increase in the direction of the main trend and shrink on the counter-move. A breakout on shrinking participation is suspect.

Our addition, because we are trading and not writing letters in 1902: a move that volume does not confirm is not a signal. It is a watch item.
Say plainly that Dow never wrote a book; these are the letters, edited by Robert Rhea (1922) and Hamilton. It helps people take "130-year-old idea" seriously when you add that every modern trend filter is a restatement of it. Also flag what Dow does not say: it never promised a mechanical buy point, and it explicitly expects the secondary move to be the one that hurts you.
Part 1 - price discounts everything

The news is not the event. The price action after the event is the news.

What the axiom buys you

  • Every man-made event - elections, budgets, RBI policy, quarterly results, tariff headlines - is known to enough people that it is priced in early. By the time you read it, the move is largely done.
  • Natural disasters and genuinely unforeseeable shocks are the exception: they cause the knee-jerk dip, and the dip recovers, because nobody had time to position.
  • So your edge is never "I knew the news". Your edge is reading what price did with the news.
If you cannot find the reason in the price, the reason is not the point. The close is.

Consequence for how we label a chart

  • Structure is built from swing highs and lows - visibly, not by an indicator.
  • A pivot only counts once price has moved away from it for the required number of bars. Before that it is unconfirmed, and we do not trade it.
  • A pivot that only the wick touched is worth less than one the close defended. That is the close-weighting rule again, one layer down.

On the desk this is the toggle Close-weighted pivots and the label column Weight in the swings table. Wick-only pivots are still drawn, but they do not get to be a level you place a stop behind.

Part 1 - the closing price

Daily close gets the most weight. Always check it before any trade decision - even intraday.

The open is an intention. The intraday range is an argument. The close is the settlement.

Why the close

It is the one price at which every participant, including the ones with the most money, has had the whole session to change their mind - and where index and derivative settlement actually happens.

Intraday does not exempt you

Even if you are scalping a 5-minute chart, you first ask what yesterday's close did and where today's close is likely to fall. The intraday move inherits the daily one.

The mistake this kills

Selling because of a mid-session spike, or buying a low that was never a level. The extreme touches; the close decides.

Drill this: ask the room "what is the close?" before you let anybody say a number out loud for the rest of the course. On the desk, the readout that enforces it is Acceptance (yes / no) on every event, and Close accept in the swings table.

Part 1 - priced early vs knee-jerk

The same rule, three different events

Sugar / ethanol, Jan-Feb 2026

Sugar-linked stocks bottomed in January / February, before the Hormuz closure moved the ethanol story. Nobody can use that in a trade - but it is the proof of the axiom: the money that could act, acted early, and the "obvious" catalyst arrived into a price that had already turned.

Verify in class: desk, custom window, AMDIND.NS / BALRAMCHEM.NS, 1D bars, Jan-Jun - mark the low before you mention the event.

Nifty, March 2020 lockdown

The genuine shock. Lockdown announced Fri 20 Mar 2020, effective the 23rd. Index low in this window printed 7,511.10 on 24 Mar; close basis bottomed 7,610.25 on 23 Mar. Then 8,317.85 on 25 Mar - +9.3% in two sessions from the panic close.

A natural-disaster-class shock makes the knee-jerk dip and recovers. That is exactly what the tape did.

Elections, June 2024

Man-made, expected, dated in advance - and the surprise result still produced a close 600 points off the low and a full recovery the next day.

Compare the two shapes: election day was a one-way auction that settled; 2020 was a capitulation that reversed. Different trade plans, and the difference is visible in the bars.

The 2020 numbers above are from this feed's ^NSEI daily series (9-31 Mar 2020): low of window 7,511.10 on 24 Mar, close low 7,610.25 on 23 Mar, and 8,317.85 on 25 Mar. Do not quote a different "2020 bottom" from memory in the same breath - pick one source and stay inside it for the hour.
Part 1 - the discipline

Stop collecting reasons. Reasons are how losses get justified.

  • Price first, story second, and if the story contradicts the price, drop the story.
  • Searching for the "why" after a loss feels like work. It is usually the beginning of an excuse: "it fell because of FII selling" tells you nothing about the next bar and frees you to repeat the trade.
  • The cause-hunt has a worse cost: it moves your exit. You start holding a broken structure because "fundamentally nothing changed". Fundamentally, the close did change. That is the signal.
  • Journal column is what price did, not what I read.

Where news legitimately belongs

As a calendar, never as a forecast. You already know the dates: RBI policy, the monthly auto sales releases, the budget, quarterly results. Those dates are when an already compressed price gets its chance to expand. Mark the date, form no view on the outcome, and trade the reaction off the close.

Desk: put the window on the instrument, note the range compression in the readout, then wait for the acceptance decision on the day.

Q&A block 1 - before we move to trends

Questions I expect on using price over news

If price discounts everything, why do I need to know anything about the company?

You need to know what is trading - liquidity, circuit limits, expiry behaviour, the session it respects. You do not need the thesis. The thesis is what the people on the other side of your trade already paid for.

Results are out and the stock fell 8%. The numbers were fine. Why?

Because auto companies publish monthly sales on the 1st, so by the time the quarterly number arrives, volumes - the part that actually moves an auto or FMCG print - have been known for three months. The "surprise" was priced in three separate installments. Check the structure before the print, not the print.

But RBI / budget moves the market. That is news causing price.

Track the policy calendar. Events act as triggers for price that is already compressed - they decide when an expansion starts, not whether the trend was there. A coin-flip release into a 4-week contracting range is a setup. The same release after a 30% run is a distribution day.

Give us the scanner. And the indicator settings. And the book list.

They exist and they are ready. I release them one topic at a time, because a scanner in front of someone who cannot label a higher low is a way to lose money faster. This week is the eye. The tools follow the eye.

So never read the news?

Read it for the calendar and for the universe (what is even in play). Never for a reason to hold. The moment you can name the reason you are holding, ask whether that reason was in the price before you formed it.

What if the close is manipulated / gap up at the open ruins my level?

The close is the settlement, not the auction, so it is the least gameable single number in the day - and the one that decides the daily chart everyone else is watching. Gaps get their own session; today's rule still applies: the first bar after a gap is information, the close of the day is the instruction.

If the room goes quiet, ask them for a loss they can explain with news. Then ask what the close did on that day. This lands harder than anything on this slide.
Part 2 - the three trends

Same direction, three different clocks

Primary · the tide

monthly chart · runs for years

This is the trend you are allowed to be long into. India's is bullish: about 2,800 in the 2008 crisis window (Oct 2008 close 2,755.10) to about 23,000 today (last close 22,597.50, Oct 2026).

Roughly 8x across the period, with two multi-year declines inside it that felt like the end of the market at the time.

Secondary · the correction

18 to 24 months, runs against the primary

The long counter-move inside the tide. Nifty is currently in a secondary downtrend: peak close 26,203 (Oct 2025) to 22,597 (Oct 2026), about -14%, on the monthly bars.

Trade it, but never call it the trend. This is the one that makes people say "the bull run is over".

Minor · the ripple

under 3 weeks · daily chart, 6+ months of data

What you actually enter on. Only taken in the direction of the primary. Under three weeks, and it is noise if you look at it on a 3-minute chart.

Insist on the wording: primary is what you believe, minor is what you trade, secondary is what tries to separate you from both. Also say out loud that a monthly chart of India starting around 2007 is all this feed gives us - anything older is somebody's memory, including mine.
Part 2 - the definition you will use every day

Higher highs and higher lows, or get out of the way

schematic Uptrend = higher high + higher low, both confirmed by the close. The dashed line is the fitted trendline through the close-backed lows.
schematic Downtrend = lower high + lower low. Stay away from longs in it - the course says stay away, not "wait for a reason".

Four labels, no more. HH, HL, LH, LL - plus EQH / EQL when the level is roughly flat. If a swing cannot be labelled, it is not a swing yet.

Equal is not new. Two highs at the same price is a level being tested. Repeated tests on falling volume is where breakdowns come from; that is Part 3.

Part 2 - how to use the three trends

One rule, and the chart sizes it forbids

Trade the minor trend only in the direction of the primary trend. That single sentence is a position-sizing rule, an exit rule and a "do nothing" rule at the same time.

Part 2 - when the answer is "no trade"

Voltas: the correct decision was to be an observer

The pattern

Highs and lows that refuse to align - a higher high with a higher low that the close does not defend, or a lower high against a higher low. The structure is mixed, and mixed is not a signal in either direction. It is information that you do not have an edge today.

On the desk, mixed prints as a compression or contraction title with a middling score, and the pending list fills up with items whose reasons read "acceptance run 1 of 2 bars".

Why staying flat is a position

Observing costs nothing and keeps your size available for the clean one. Every forced trade inside a mixed structure borrows from the capital and the attention you will need when the trend resolves.

Say the sentence exactly: "I am allowed to not have an opinion." Most of the room will hear that for the first time.

Live demo if you want the real bar: desk, VOLTAS.NS, chip 1D - 6M, then walk the swings table and show that the last high and last low disagree. Do not name a price target for it. The point of the Voltas example is the absence of a decision, not a call.
Part 2 - reading your own chart honestly

Four failure modes, in the order people meet them

"I see higher highs but no higher lows."

That is not an uptrend, that is an uptrend with a widening base. It is a warning, and it usually resolves down. Require both sides - which is exactly why the desk refuses to publish a bull score unless there is a readable pair on each side.

"The chart looks the same at 1D-1M and 1Y-5W."

Then you have not zoomed out far enough to see which trend you are in. Always label on the 6-12 month daily tape first, then decide the entry on the minor.

"The wick made a new high, so it is a higher high, right?"

Only if the close defends it. Wick-only pivots are drawn and tagged, but they do not become the level you hide a stop behind. That is the close-weighting rule doing its job.

"Can I mark the trend myself with two points?"

Not on this course. Two arbitrary points fit any tape. Our trendline goes through close-backed swing pivots - at most the last six on each side - and it needs an R-squared of 0.5 or better to be drawn at all. Below that, the readout says so instead of flattering you.

Part 3 - volume confirms the trend

The rule, stated the way you will use it

Volume should rise when price takes out a previous high or a previous low. If it does not, the break is a candidate, not a signal.

Confirming a breakout

Price closes above the prior swing high and volume expands versus the bars immediately before it. Then hold: the follow-through has to keep the level on closes, not on one bar.

Confirming a breakdown

Same test, downward: a close through the prior swing low on expanding volume. Breakdowns are quicker than breakouts - fear needs less participation than greed.

The false break

Price pokes through the level, volume does not cooperate, and the close comes back inside. Now you own the best short of the day (or the best exit of a long). Failed break > no trade.

How to judge volume without a formula

Look at the volume bar and compare it with the last 5 to 6 bars by eye. Is it clearly taller than the neighbours? That is the whole test, and it works on any charting platform in the world. Our desk formalises it as RVOL - volume divided by the median of the last 20 bars - because eyeballing drifts when the tape is quiet, and because a number can be argued about while a feeling cannot.

Ignore the colour of the volume bar

Green and red volume bars are an app gimmick. They are typically coloured by the bar's own open-to-close direction, which tells you about the body of one candle and nothing about participation. Two identical 3-million-share days get painted opposite colours depending on a 40-point range. Judge height, never hue.

Part 3 - the shape of the two cases

True breakout versus false breakout

schematic Prior high taken out on a close, with volume bars stepping above their neighbours. The follow-through keeps the level.
schematic Same price poke, smaller volume than the bars before it, and the close back inside the range. That is a trap, and it is a trade.

What to actually do with the difference. Confirmed: enter on the close or the next bar's retest, stop behind the broken level, target measured off the prior range. Unconfirmed: no entry - it goes on the watch list, and if the next two closes come back inside, you are allowed to fade it. Never confuse the two states in the journal; they are different trades with different sizes.

Part 3 - duration tells you which trend you are in

A 1-2 year decline is a correction. A bear market lasts many years.

The confusion that wrecks portfolios

A stock (or an index) that has come down for 1 to 2 years while its primary trend is still bullish is in a secondary move. It is not the end of anything. People who call it a bear market sell the bottom of a tide that is still up - Nifty Auto above: -28% in 192 days, then +7.9% in eight sessions off the bottom close.

What a real bear market looks like

A bear is measured in many years, with the primary trend itself turning down: lower highs and lower lows on the monthly bars, not the daily ones. That is the Arcom-class example - a decline long enough that the monthly structure changes its labels, and the correct answer for the whole period is "stay away", not "buy the dip".

Part 3 - volume practice

Three drills on real tape, in this order

Drill 1 - eyeball it first

Pull up any chart. Cover the numbers. Ask: is the volume bar at that break clearly taller than the five before it? Yes or no, from the eye. Then and only then read RVOL. The eye has to work without the crutch - it is the only tool that transfers to a broker terminal.

Drill 2 - find the failed one

Scroll until you find a break of a prior high where volume was flat or lower and the close came back inside. Mark it. Now find the next bar. Most of these give you a bigger move in the opposite direction than the break itself did.

Drill 3 - chase the persistence

A high RVOL bar means nothing if the following two closes fall back inside the range. The desk counts this explicitly (sustain). Find a thrust where volume spiked but nothing held - that is a one-day event, and it is not a trend.

Run drill 1 live with the room. Do not narrate the answer; ask two people for theirs before you show RVOL. The disagreement you will hear is the reason the metric exists.
Part 3 - the one-page summary of volume

Six sentences

1. Volume confirms direction; it does not create it.
  • 2. Test it at a break of a prior swing high or low, not in the middle of a range.
  • 3. Compare against the last 5-6 bars by eye; RVOL against a 20-bar median to be arguable.
  • 4. The close must accept the level. Wick through it is a probe.
  • 5. Two more closes to keep it - one bar is a spike, not a run.
  • 6. Unconfirmed means no position. It does not mean "small position".

Open Q&A

Questions on the whole of today

Dow Theory says the trend is intact until a clear reversal. Where is my line?

A confirmed close through the prior swing low on expanding volume. Not a price you liked, not a news item. If you cannot name the bar that would prove you wrong on the chart, you are not in a trend trade - you are in a belief.

What if both trendlines point opposite ways?

Converging lines are a compression, and compression is a when not a which way. Wait for the break of one of them with volume. This is the Voltas state - be an observer.

Is a lower high in a primary uptrend a sell?

It is a reason to stop adding and to move your exit closer. A single lower high is not a reversed primary trend; two of them plus a lower low is a secondary decline.

How much of this is subjective? Two people labelled the same chart differently today.

A lot, at first - which is why we fix the parameters and keep them for the whole course: pivot width, the close weight, the volume threshold, the two-bar acceptance. Disagreement between two people with the same rules is a discussion. Disagreement between two people with different rules is worthless.

Can I use this on futures / crypto / US names?

The three axioms travel. The details do not: crypto has no settlement close and no session, so the close-weight rule needs replacing; US has pre/post that pollutes pivots. Use it on indices and large-cap equities first.

Write these six lines before you close the laptop

2. The close is the vote. Check it before every decision, even intraday.

6. Volume must confirm the break. Unconfirmed is not a signal.
